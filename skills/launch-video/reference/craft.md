# Craft: laws, tones, shape and the review list

The product's own rules (BRAND.md) win over anything here.

## Laws

- **Short.** 15–25 s for social; 18–22 is the sweet spot. Landing loops may run to 60 s.
- **Clear to a stranger.** After one viewing, someone who has never heard of it knows what it does, who it's for, and how to get it. Lead with that, not with how it's built.
- **The hook is everything.** The first 2 seconds decide whether anyone keeps watching. Something moves in the first second.
- **Show the thing.** The working product doing its job beats a landing page describing it. Small illustrative UI text is fine (a name, a toast); invented numbers, testimonials or features are not.
- **Specific.** Its own copy and claims. Generic SaaS language ("streamline your workflow") marks the video as made for nobody.
- **Readable.** Pace comes from motion and cuts. A line meant to be read stays fully visible and settled 0.3 s per word, counted from when the whole line is on screen. Fewer words beat smaller words; cut labels that restate the picture.
- **Alive.** Things appear one by one, clicks, taps, swipes and typing. Something happens on every beat.
- **Only the product's surfaces.** Its backgrounds, borders, radii and effects — no invented cards, glows, particles or bouncy easing its UI doesn't use.
- **Every frame postable.** Any frozen frame is worth sharing.
- **Funny earns its place** — from the product's own absurdity, never from trying.

## Shape

Hook (2–3 s) → Reveal: what it is (2–4 s) → 2–3 highlights, one idea each → Punchline / ending: tagline, URL or CTA (2–4 s). A starting shape, not a template. If the user named one feature or release, the whole video is about that.

Punchlines between scenes: at most 6 words, 2 lines. Captions: one short line in a fixed band. Inside scenes, only the UI's own text.

## Tones

Freeform direction ("fake Series A launch from 2016") refines or overrides a preset.

| Tone | Feel | Pacing / transitions |
|---|---|---|
| `default` | Punchy, playful, clean | 4–5 scenes; soft transitions |
| `polished` | Serious, elegant, restrained | 3–4 scenes, long holds; soft fades |
| `yc-parody` | Deadpan startup launch, played straight | 4–5 scenes, one claim each; hard cuts |
| `chaotic` | FAST, LOUD, ALL CAPS | 6–8 scenes, some under 2 s; flash/zoom cuts |
| `deadpan` | Calm, dry, nothing is a joke | 3–4 scenes, big empty space; slow fades |
| `cinematic` | Trailer-scale, epic claims | 4–5 scenes, big type; dramatic wipes |
| `app-store` | Clean feature cards | 4–6 scenes; smooth slides |

## Transitions

- **Cut on the beat** — hard cut exactly on a beat; match background and accent across it.
- **Magic move** — one element present in both scenes (a button, a row, a logo) travels to its new place; the rest swaps out. Measure both ends. Images and marks travel; words blur out and back in.
- **Crossfade** only between calm layouts. Two busy layouts crossfaded make a muddy double exposure: stagger it (old out, then new in) or dip through the background.

## Review list (every pass)

- **Text:** fully on frame, readable at delivery size (UI 24 px+ at 1080p), never covered by a cursor or chip, never crossing other text, never re-centering while it builds (keep each word's slot).
- **Reading time:** every line meant to be read holds as long as the script's Read column says.
- **Color:** one background; only the product's surfaces and borders; contrast holds.
- **Transitions:** no muddy double exposures; every traveller lands exactly on its target.
- **Pacing:** no dead beat; nothing too fast to read.
- **Claims:** every word on screen matches BRAND.md's claims list; nothing the product removed or never did.
- **Loading states** keep their width.
